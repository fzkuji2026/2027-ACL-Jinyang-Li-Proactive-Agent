\section{Memory Implementation Details}
\label{app:implementation}

This appendix expands the evidence lifecycle in Section~\ref{sec:framework}; the core acceptance conditions and causal interfaces remain in the main paper.

\subsection{Daily Buffer and Checkable Fields}
Daily construction uses an independent buffer of committed events for the open local day, so Event Memory truncation does not discard construction evidence.
The buffer is cleared after that day is processed.
Each daily segment records a local interval, typed \texttt{observed}, \texttt{context-before}, and \texttt{outcome} fields, and supporting event identifiers.
These activity segments are distinct from service episodes used for intervention control.
Scalar values are canonicalized; field paths must agree exactly or through a schema-preserving suffix.
The validator also checks entity, interval, and citation consistency.
Unsupported facts and unresolved or cross-entity citations are rejected.
Free-text descriptions do not substitute for typed evidence in validation or replay.
Accepted segments form daily trajectories retained in the current construction cycle, even after their source events leave Event Memory.

\subsection{Construction Clock and Replay Boundaries}
A weekly cycle is a non-overlapping, time-ordered block of seven completed daily-trajectory records for one entity, not a fixed calendar week.
Dates without a daily record are not filled in, so a cycle may span more than seven calendar days.
Empty or unobservable records count toward cycle completion but provide no fulfilled or violated replay evidence.
After consolidation, the online daily-memory view starts a new cycle, while daily records remain available for audit.

A proposal specifies trigger context $g$, expected pattern $p$, a local-time window, optional weekday restrictions, and supporting daily identifiers.
It describes an expectation, not a recommendation to intervene.
Replay excludes days outside applicability conditions and censors missing observations.
An observable day with a matching trigger is fulfilled if $p$ occurs at or after the trigger within the specified window; otherwise it is violated.
Each date contributes at most once.
Censored days do not enter the confidence denominator in Equation~\ref{eq:replay_gate}.
Confidence is zero without eligible days; lift is zero when the base rate is zero.
The base rate uses observable days under the same window and weekday restrictions, without conditioning on $g$.

\subsection{Pattern Lifecycle}
\label{app:lifecycle}
Lifecycle status controls retrieval eligibility, separately from current occurrence and service-episode states.
After admission, fulfilled and violated observations update stored confidence through a smoothed support ratio combining replay counts and subsequent outcomes with one initial success and one initial failure.
Censored observations supply neither success nor failure evidence.
Two consecutive violations move an Active pattern to \emph{weakening}, which remains retrievable at lower priority; four move it to \emph{dormant}, which is excluded.
A fulfilled observation restores a weakening pattern to Active when updated confidence is at least $0.5$.
A dormant pattern returns only to Candidate and requires replay readmission.
Explicitly retired patterns and all Candidates are excluded from Full \system{} retrieval.

\subsection{Current Occurrence Matching}
\label{app:matching}
A deterministic matcher checks trigger context, weekday restrictions, and expected windows using a 30-minute lead and grace interval.
Triggers may be current or previously recorded causal occurrences.
Each occurrence has one of five states: not-started, pending, fulfilled, deviated, or censored.
Fulfilled and censored occurrences are excluded.
The remainder are ranked by occurrence state (deviated, pending, then not-started), lifecycle priority, stored confidence, and temporal distance; at most three patterns enter $R_t$.
All matching and state assessment use only the current event and causally available observations.
Daily Trajectory Memory supplies the current cycle's completed daily trajectories rather than a similarity-ranked subset.

\subsection{Episode Identity and Causal Execution}
\label{app:episode_control}
The controller combines observable scope-specific state, service intent, and cited pattern identifiers.
State includes availability/activity context for AS, activity/room context for AL, and repository activity/target identifiers for RA.
AL additionally distinguishes onset, confirmed, and sustained evidence phases.
Changed episode state, stronger evidence, changed service or pattern evidence, or a new local day permits reconsideration without automatically producing an intervention.
The same controller is used for ProactiveAgent, Mem0, Event-only, Event+Daily, and Full \system{}, except when explicitly ablated.

The causal execution order is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prepare prior memory; any due maintenance uses only previously committed events.
\item Stage $e_t$ without committing it, then select daily and temporal evidence.
\item Obtain the decision LLM's proposal from the structured context.
\item Apply repeated-opportunity suppression and finalize the output.
\item Commit $e_t$ and update coverage state; maintenance incorporating $e_t$ affects only later decisions.
\end{enumerate}
Reference labels are joined only for offline scoring.

\section{Public Events and Reference-Label Protocol}
\label{app:benchmark_protocol}

\subsection{Scopes and Public Events}

\paragraph{Activity Support.}
This scope derives from HeartSteps mobile activity contexts~\citep{klasnja2019heartsteps}.
We retain 1,456 meta-events from nine complete participant streams, covering 367 observed participant-days.
Complete streams preserve the chronology required for daily and longer-term memory construction without sampling within a participant.
Public fields include decision-time availability, recognized activity, recent step summaries, location category, weather, and local decision slot.
Randomized treatment assignments and future behavioral outcomes are excluded.
Reference labels concern opportunities for optional activity support, rather than reproduction of the original intervention assignment or estimation of health effects.

\paragraph{Ambient Living.}
This scope derives from CASAS ambient-sensor traces~\citep{cook2013casas} and contains four homes with 14 consecutive days each.
Within every local day, ten-minute preprocessing blocks are merged into 28 regular 50-minute segments and one 40-minute tail, producing 406 events per home.
Every source block is assigned to exactly one segment, preserving the complete two-week chronology.
A segment becomes available only after its interval closes and contains within-interval motion counts, room sequences, door actions, and compact numeric summaries.
It neither summarizes earlier segments nor exposes a personal baseline, deviation score, or candidate judgment; historical evidence must therefore be recovered by the evaluated agent.
The service space is restricted to gentle, low-risk check-ins and excludes disease, fall, travel, danger, and unsupported preference inference.

\paragraph{Repository Assistance.}
This scope combines public GH Archive records~\citep{gharchive} and constructed repository events, yielding 1,459 events across four repositories.
All records retain their repository-local temporal order.
The repository stream is the memory owner and evaluation unit, rather than an individual contributor.
Services include low-risk triage, review, and maintenance assistance.
The native GitHub type \texttt{PushEvent} denotes repository activity and is unrelated to an intervention decision.

\paragraph{Public-input contract.}
Reference-positive and reference-negative events follow the same scope-specific public schema.
Public inputs contain event identifiers, stream identifiers, timestamps, and contemporaneously observable fields, but no reference label, candidate flag, episode annotation, trajectory summary, memory-level marker, or scoring mask.
The inventory includes early events observed before Daily Trajectory Memory or Temporal Pattern Memory becomes available.

\subsection{Reference Labels and Causal Replay}
\label{app:reference_protocol}

\paragraph{Service-opportunity semantics.}
Annotators assess the current event $e_t$ and the complete strictly prior public history $\mathcal{H}_{<t}=(e_1,\ldots,e_{t-1})$ of the same entity, independently of the memory representation retained by a tested method.
Let $A(e_t)$ indicate that the current event itself establishes a concrete, timely, feasible, and low-risk service opportunity, and let $B(e_t,\mathcal{H}_{<t})$ indicate that prior evidence makes such an opportunity visible at the current event.
Let $N_t^{\mathrm{ref}}$ indicate opportunity novelty under the reference episode protocol below.
The reviewed label follows Equation~\ref{eq:or_semantics}.
These predicates specify annotation semantics, rather than learned classifiers or additional decision gates.
Historical deviation is neither necessary nor sufficient: an event may be actionable without a deviation, while an unusual event may still lack a useful or safe service action.
Within a semantic episode, the first reliable and actionable window is labeled \textsc{Intervene}.
Subsequent covered windows are labeled \textsc{Abstain} unless the evidence strengthens, the feasible service changes, or the episode reopens.
Reference episode coverage, and hence $N_t^{\mathrm{ref}}$, is determined from the reviewed opportunity sequence up to the current event rather than from a method's predicted interventions.
The labels consequently represent reviewed service opportunities, not recipient acceptance, treatment effects, or an optimal deployment policy.

\paragraph{Annotation protocol.}
An LLM-assisted screening stage identifies potential service opportunities and expands the review queue.
Three team members then independently assign initial labels without access to the evaluated systems' decisions, followed by team cross-review and adjudication.
Every public event enters final review, including events not selected during screening.
The screening output can prioritize inspection but cannot determine a reference label automatically.
We use protocol-fixed evaluation rather than claim an independent development/test split.
The Ambient Living aggregation rule is specified before its event rows are constructed, and each evaluation version jointly records its event inventory, reference-label map, intervention prompt, Event Memory boundary, and validation settings.

\paragraph{Causal replay.}
Every participant, home, or repository stream is replayed independently from empty method state and in timestamp order.
At event $t$, a method may access the current event $e_t$, its own strictly prior same-stream memory, and any preceding outputs retained by its architecture.
It may not access another stream, a future event, an evaluator-private label, or a summary computed from the complete sequence.
The intervention decision is finalized before $e_t$ is committed to memory.
Daily and weekly maintenance involving $e_t$ can therefore affect only later decisions.
Reference labels are joined after inference for scoring and never enter memory construction or retrieval.
Annotation artifacts, reference episode states, and scoring metadata remain organizer-private.

\section{Additional Experimental Configuration}
\label{app:configuration}

\paragraph{External baselines.}
\emph{ProactiveAgent} adapts ProactiveAgent's released evaluation prompt and proposal/null output contract~\citep{lu2025proactiveagent,thunlp2026proactiveagentrepo}, without its fine-tuned checkpoint.
It retains causal event--decision history, capped at 1,000 events including the current event per request, without external structured memory.
\emph{Mem0} uses the open-source \texttt{mem0ai==2.0.12} extraction, consolidation, and retrieval pipeline~\citep{chhikara2025mem0,mem0ai2026mem0v2012}, local \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} embeddings, top-$8$ same-entity retrieval, and a $0.1$ search threshold.
The baselines retain their method-specific memory and prompt interfaces while sharing the task semantics and final Episode Controller.
This comparison does not impose equal context or token budgets.

\paragraph{Memory variants and retrieval control.}
\emph{Event-only} receives the current event and bounded Event Memory (Equation~\ref{eq:source_memory}).
\emph{Event+Daily} additionally receives fact-validated daily trajectories; Full \system{} also receives eligible temporal patterns (Equation~\ref{eq:long_term_retrieval}).
These three variants share the intervention prompt, output schema, and Episode Controller, differing in available memory evidence.
For Activity Support and Ambient Living, \emph{Flat Retrieval Memory} adds to Event-only the three most similar older same-entity events outside its window.
It uses fixed dense embeddings and cosine similarity, without daily consolidation, replay, neighbor expansion, or LLM reranking.

\paragraph{Mechanism ablations.}
Section~\ref{sec:validation_ablations} defines the three component removals and their controls; Table~\ref{tab:validation_ablations_full} retains all reported metrics.
No other component changes within each ablation.

\paragraph{Model roles and implementation.}
In the primary setting, decision and memory-construction calls use \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} through the official OpenAI API, with temperature zero and thinking disabled.
Decision generation and memory construction are separate call roles, not a single shared output budget.
Decision limits are interface-specific (256 or 512 tokens); construction-call limits are configured separately and recorded by role in the run configuration.
Event Memory contains at most 99 prior events within 72 hours, plus the separately supplied current event.
Daily construction follows local-day closure; weekly construction and replay follow seven completed daily records as defined in Section~\ref{sec:framework}.
All memory and validation records are entity-scoped.

We also test \texttt{gpt-5.6-terra} and \texttt{gpt-5.6-sol}.
Both decision and construction LLMs change together; Appendix~\ref{app:backbone_sensitivity} provides the fixed settings and complete results.
Input manifests, prompts, schemas, thresholds, and role-specific configurations are versioned in the experiment ledger.

\paragraph{Scoring.}
Section~\ref{sec:experimental_settings} defines the scoring policy and metrics.
Intervention Rate measures decision frequency, not perceived interruption burden.

\subsection{Intervention-Rate Interpretation}
\label{app:rate_interpretation}
Intervention Rate is a behavioral operating point, not a standalone performance target.
Writing $\rho$ for reference-positive prevalence, the exact identity $\mathrm{IR}=\rho\,\mathrm{Recall}+(1-\rho)\,\mathrm{FIR}$ separates opportunity recovery from false intervention, with all rates expressed as fractions.
The prevalences are 25.1\%, 11.7\%, and 11.9\% in AS, AL, and RA.
However, prevalence alone cannot explain the variation: ProactiveAgent intervenes at 9.2\% of AL events but 53.1\% of RA events despite their similar prevalences.
Their respective recall and false-intervention rates reveal different coverage--selectivity operating points under the scope-specific evidence and service semantics.
This descriptive decomposition does not isolate an intrinsic LLM proactivity trait from memory, prompt, and controller effects.
