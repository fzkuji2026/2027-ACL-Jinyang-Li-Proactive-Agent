\section{Memory-Construction Audit and Online Availability}
\label{app:construction_audit}

Complementing the decision-quality ablations in Section~\ref{sec:validation_ablations}, we audit existing memory-construction artifacts and maintenance logs from Activity Support and Ambient Living, covering nine participants and four homes.
Table~\ref{tab:validation_audit} distinguishes validation and replay operations from unique retained artifacts.
The audit checks source support, entity consistency, and replay consistency rather than intervention outcomes.

\begin{table*}[t]
\centering
\small
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{5.0pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.22\textwidth}p{0.28\textwidth}p{0.45\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
View & Counting unit & Audit result \\
\midrule
Daily validation log & validation attempt & 311 rejected before storage \\
Retained daily store & unique evidence-linked segment & 1,279 checked; all source-supported; 0 cross-entity \\
Weekly replay log & replay attempt & 140 attempts; 118 pass; 22 not activated at that update \\
Retained pattern store & unique temporal-pattern proposal & 54 checked; 42 Active; 12 Candidate; 0 inconsistent replays \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Memory-construction audit for Activity Support and Ambient Living (nine participants and four homes). Log rows count maintenance operations; store rows count unique retained artifacts.}
\label{tab:validation_audit}
\end{table*}

\paragraph{Retained evidence.}
The daily store contains 423 trajectories---367 from Activity Support and 56 from Ambient Living---comprising 1,279 retained segments.
All retained segments pass the configured source-support and entity-consistency checks, with no cross-entity evidence.
Separately, construction logs record 311 rejected daily validation checks.
These observations characterize filtering and the retained evidence, rather than an error-free construction LLM.

The final temporal-pattern store contains 54 source-linked proposals whose recorded replay results are consistent with offline recomputation: 42 are Active and 12 remain Candidate.
Under Full \system{}, Candidates remain excluded from decision evidence.
Across weekly maintenance updates, 118 of 140 replay attempts pass the activation gate, while 22 do not.
These operation counts are distinct from the 42 unique Active patterns in the final store.

\paragraph{Online availability.}
Daily Trajectory Memory first becomes available for all 13 entities at a median elapsed time of 0.80 days.
An Active temporal pattern first becomes available for 11 entities at a median of 6.79 days, whereas relevant temporal-pattern evidence is first matched for eight entities at a median of 10.46 days.
Each median is calculated over entities reaching the corresponding stage.
Pattern activation and current relevance are therefore distinct: an admitted pattern does not automatically enter every decision context.

These observations show that daily and temporal-pattern evidence can become available within the evaluated streams, leaving subsequent events for evidence-informed decisions.
Causal validity follows from the update order in Section~3.3: evidence used to judge an event is constructed from previously committed events.
Together with Section~\ref{sec:validation_ablations}, this audit addresses RQ3 by separating evidence integrity and availability from downstream intervention quality.

The inventory also includes two false positives and two false negatives from Event-only logs.
In Activity Support, an activity suggestion is issued despite substantial movement during the preceding hour, while another low-activity event with a positive reference label receives ABSTAIN.
In Ambient Living, ordinary bedroom--bathroom movement receives a check-in, whereas a reference-positive quiet period spanning two consecutive segments is missed.

These errors illustrate the distinction between an observable condition and an actionable service opportunity.
Current inactivity or room transitions alone may be insufficient to justify assistance, but conservative interpretation can also miss reviewed opportunities.
Historical evidence can contextualize such observations without determining the intervention decision by itself.
Repeated windows additionally require assessing whether evidence or service needs have changed, complementing the controller trade-off examined in Section~\ref{sec:validation_ablations}.

This qualitative analysis addresses the failure-analysis part of RQ4 and illustrates why \system{} separates memory evidence, present service value, and prior opportunity coverage.
