\paragraph{Retained evidence.}
The daily store contains 423 trajectories---367 from Activity Support and 56 from Ambient Living---comprising 1,279 retained segments.
All retained segments pass the configured source-support and entity-consistency checks, with no cross-entity evidence.
Separately, construction logs record 311 rejected daily validation checks.
These observations characterize filtering and the retained evidence, rather than an error-free construction LLM.

The final temporal-pattern store contains 54 source-linked proposals whose recorded replay results are consistent with offline recomputation: 42 are Active and 12 remain Candidate.
Under \system{}, Candidates remain excluded from decision evidence.
Across weekly maintenance updates, 118 of 140 replay attempts pass the activation gate, while 22 do not.
These operation counts are distinct from the 42 unique Active patterns in the final store.

\paragraph{Online availability.}
Daily Trajectory Memory first becomes available for all 13 entities at a median elapsed time of 0.80 days.
An Active temporal pattern first becomes available for 11 entities at a median of 6.79 days, whereas relevant temporal-pattern evidence is first matched for eight entities at a median of 10.46 days.
Each median is calculated over entities reaching the corresponding stage.
Pattern activation and current relevance are therefore distinct: an admitted pattern does not automatically enter every decision context.

These observations show that daily and temporal-pattern evidence can become available within the evaluated streams, leaving subsequent events for evidence-informed decisions.
The update order in Section~\ref{sec:decision_control} preserves the causal boundary: evidence used to judge an event is constructed from previously committed events.
Together with Section~\ref{sec:validation_ablations}, this audit addresses RQ3 by separating evidence integrity and availability from downstream service-opportunity detection performance.

\section{Independent Human Review Across Three Scopes}
\label{app:label_review_450}

We conduct a sampled label review within the main benchmark, selecting 150 consecutive target events per scope (450 in total).
A separate three-reviewer team independently annotates these segments without access to either ProEviMem or CSM outputs, and discusses its judgments to obtain consensus references.
Each segment belongs to exactly one participant, home, or project repository; no segment mixes entities.
A fixed SHA256-based rule first ranks eligible streams and then eligible contiguous starts using public metadata only, without reference labels or predictions, and requires at least seven days of preceding history.
The selected streams are AS participant\_35, AL home\_0004, and the RA repository \texttt{callstack/react-native-pager-view}.
Their target periods are December 16, 2015--January 18, 2016; June 23--28, 2011; and June 21--November 4, 2025, respectively.
All 37, 203, and 152 preceding events are retained and processed chronologically by the corresponding ProEviMem pipeline, but excluded from scoring.
Thus, 842 public events supply causal context for 450 scored targets; each target can use only its current observation and strictly prior same-stream history.

The reviewers inspect each target with its preceding same-stream history, rather than judging isolated events.
The confirmed review yields 27/123, 15/135, and 34/116 \textsc{Intervene}/\textsc{Abstain} labels for AS, AL, and RA, respectively.
Agreement with the earlier diagnostic references is 88.7\% (133/150), 91.3\% (137/150), and 85.3\% (128/150) for AS, AL, and RA, respectively.
In AS and AL, 13 and 13 labels change from \textsc{Intervene} to \textsc{Abstain}, compared with 4 and 0 changes in the reverse direction; RA has 6 removals and 16 additions.
Most judgments therefore agree, while service-opportunity boundaries remain consequential.
These counts characterize differences between label versions, not pre-discussion inter-annotator agreement or the semantic cause of each disagreement.
These annotations serve as references for the additional evaluation; the main benchmark labels and result tables remain unchanged.
For model comparison, we use the recorded ProEviMem and CSM decisions from the selected-segment diagnostic runs, rather than a subset of the main-run output files.
For each method, exactly the same target decisions are scored against both label versions; changing the reference labels does not rerun inference or alter the outputs.
Like the main experiment, this diagnostic uses \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} through the official OpenAI API, with temperature zero and thinking disabled.
Decision-output limits are 256 tokens for AS/AL and 512 for RA.
Both methods retain the 72-hour/100-event bound and episode control; ProEviMem additionally retains daily validation and replay gating.

Table~\ref{tab:label_review_450} reports ProEviMem under the confirmed event-level references, and Table~\ref{tab:paired_label_f1_450} adds CSM under both label versions.
ProEviMem identifies 24/27 AS, 15/15 AL, and 18/34 RA opportunities.
Its F1 remains above CSM in AL and RA under both versions; the small AS difference changes direction.
Thus, the paired audit reveals both agreement and label sensitivity, rather than establishing invariant method rankings.
For example, AL ProEviMem F1 changes from 68.7\% to 55.6\% when only the references change, while CSM changes from 46.4\% to 37.2\%.
These are within-segment comparisons, not changes from the full-scope main results: the sampled events and diagnostic runs differ from the main evaluation.
Across scopes, immediate and scheduled service decisions share the \textsc{Intervene} label.
The following paired episode analysis uses a separate, provisional reference set grounded in these reviewed events and their causal histories; event-label confirmation does not by itself confirm episode boundaries or message matches.

\subsection{Paired Episode-Level Behavioral Diagnosis}
\label{app:episode_review_450}

On the same 450 targets and 392 preceding events, we compare recorded CSM and \system{} decisions using a common set of 67 proposed episodes: 26 in AS, 10 in AL, and 31 in RA.
The analysis separates first effective assistance, useful follow-up, and repetition, without additional inference or changes to event labels.
Each episode specifies a service goal and object, causal evidence, onset, valid observed event positions, and any explicit deadline or closure evidence; it is not a daily trajectory or temporal-pattern record.
The reference set and semantic matches are model-assisted proposals. Six AL follow-up classifications have human confirmation; the remaining proposals are not treated as independently confirmed annotations.
We therefore report this version as a provisional, post-hoc paired diagnostic, separate from the blinded event-label review.
Validity at listed observations does not imply continuous validity between sparse events.

\paragraph{Reference and model histories.}
Fixed event labels use reference-opportunity novelty (Equation~\ref{eq:or_semantics}); episode coverage instead follows the evaluated method's actual preceding interventions.
Each method initializes coverage from its own prefix outputs, without counting prefix interventions as target outcomes or borrowing the other method's coverage history.
A target intervention must match an episode's service goal and object at a listed valid observation.
For timeliness, intended service time is the decision time plus the specified delay, with zero delay for immediate intervention.
If an explicit reference deadline exists, an intended service time beyond it receives no coverage credit.
The first timely match covers the episode.
For subsequent matches, we compare the message and available evidence with that method's preceding assistance for the same episode.
A follow-up is valuable when new causal evidence changes the actionable service or next step and the message addresses that change; rephrasing the same advice or continued status alone is redundant.
Cases requiring unobserved delivery, recipient response, or task completion are left uncertain.
Valuable follow-ups do not create extra episodes or increase coverage.
Each output credits at most one episode: among compatible references, the first uncovered one in the fixed reference order takes precedence; otherwise the first already covered one is used.
Unmatched or late outputs remain in the intervention denominator.
The original event labels and main result tables are unchanged.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Let $I$ be all target interventions, $F$ first valid interventions, $V$ valuable follow-ups, and $R$ redundant follow-ups.
Episode Coverage is covered new episodes divided by all new episodes.
Supported Intervention Precision (SIP) is $(F+V)/I$, extending the first-only Episode Precision $F/I$ without giving extra coverage credit.
Redundant Intervention Rate (RIR) is $R/I$.
Uncertain follow-ups ($U$), unmatched outputs, late outputs, and unresolved matches remain in $I$, but not in $F+V$ or $R$.
These categories describe compatibility with the proposed references, not verified recipient benefit.
Table~\ref{tab:episode_review_450} reports coverage and rates; Table~\ref{tab:episode_categories_450} retains the count decomposition.

\paragraph{Observation boundaries.}
The main diagnostic treats the first AL opportunity as target-onset; a plausible continuation from the prefix changes both methods' denominator to nine, with CSM covering 7/9 and \system{} 9/9.
Coverage describes the observed segment, not an opportunity's full future lifetime.
RA has six late CSM outputs and four late \system{} outputs; scheduled decisions are checked against explicit deadlines, not verified delivery.
Three CSM RA follow-ups have uncertain value: accepting all three would raise its SIP from 33.8\% to 38.5\%, a sensitivity scenario rather than an uncertainty interval.
Other unmatched and unresolved outputs are not exhaustively adjudicated for alternative service value.

\paragraph{Behavioral comparison.}
AS coverage is identical, while \system{} uses fewer interventions.
In AL, \system{} covers two more proposed opportunities and provides more valuable follow-ups, but also has a higher redundant-intervention rate.
In RA, CSM covers more opportunities, whereas \system{} has higher SIP with fewer interventions.
The low supported shares partly reflect outputs unmatched to this finite reference inventory; they do not establish that every remaining message is useless.
These trade-offs distinguish coverage, supported follow-up, and repetition rather than imply uniform superiority or isolate the controller's causal effect.
