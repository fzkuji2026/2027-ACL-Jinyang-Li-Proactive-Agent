\section{Memory-Construction Audit and Online Availability}
\label{app:construction_audit}

Complementing the evidence-check ablations in Section~\ref{sec:validation_ablations}, we audit existing memory-construction artifacts and maintenance logs from Sports and Home, covering nine participants and four homes.
Table~\ref{tab:validation_audit} records maintenance operations and unique retained artifacts in separate units.
The audit checks source support, entity consistency, and replay consistency.


\paragraph{Retained evidence.}
The daily store contains 423 trajectories, with 367 from Sports and 56 from Home, comprising 1,279 retained segments.
All retained segments pass the configured source-support and entity-consistency checks, with all evidence linked to the same entity.
Separately, construction logs record 311 rejected daily validation checks.

The final temporal-pattern store contains 54 source-linked proposals whose recorded replay results are consistent with offline recomputation: 42 are Active and 12 remain Candidate.
Decision contexts use lifecycle-eligible patterns that satisfy current-applicability conditions (Appendices~\ref{app:lifecycle} and~\ref{app:matching}).
Across weekly maintenance updates, 118 of 140 replay attempts pass the activation gate, while 22 remain below its thresholds.
These operation counts are distinct from the 42 unique Active patterns in the final store.

\paragraph{Online availability.}
Daily Trajectory Memory first becomes available for all 13 entities at a median elapsed time of 0.80 days.
An Active temporal pattern first becomes available for 11 entities at a median of 6.79 days.
Current-applicability matching first supplies a temporal pattern for eight entities at a median of 10.46 days.
Each median is calculated over entities reaching the corresponding stage.
Pattern activation and current relevance are checked separately: admitted patterns enter a decision context after current-applicability matching.

The stage counts summarized in Section~\ref{sec:validation_results} use different eligible-entity sets; the timings above retain those denominators.

\section{Independent Human Review Across Three Scopes}
\label{app:label_review_450}

We conduct a sampled label review within the main benchmark, selecting 150 consecutive target events per scope (450 in total).
A separate three-reviewer team independently annotates these segments while blinded to ProactMem and CSM outputs, and discusses its judgments to obtain consensus references.
Each segment belongs to exactly one participant, home, or project repository.
A fixed SHA256-based rule first ranks eligible streams and then eligible contiguous starts using public metadata only, and requires at least seven days of preceding history.
The selected streams are Sports participant\_35, Home home\_0004, and the Code repository \texttt{callstack/react-native-pager-view}.
Their target periods are December 16, 2015--January 18, 2016; June 23--28, 2011; and June 21--November 4, 2025, respectively.
The corresponding ProactMem pipeline processes all 37, 203, and 152 preceding events chronologically to provide historical context; scoring covers the 150 target events in each scope.
Thus, 842 public events supply causal context for 450 scored targets; each target can use only its current observation and strictly prior same-stream history.

The reviewers inspect each target together with its preceding same-stream history.
The confirmed review yields 27/123, 15/135, and 34/116 \textsc{Intervene}/\textsc{Abstain} labels for Sports, Home, and Code, respectively.
Agreement with the earlier diagnostic references is 88.7\% (133/150), 91.3\% (137/150), and 85.3\% (128/150) for Sports, Home, and Code, respectively.
In Sports and Home, 13 and 13 labels change from \textsc{Intervene} to \textsc{Abstain}, compared with 4 and 0 changes in the reverse direction; Code has 6 removals and 16 additions.
The reported counts quantify agreement between label versions and the directions of label changes.
These annotations serve as references for the additional evaluation; the main benchmark labels and result tables remain unchanged.
For model comparison, we use the recorded ProactMem and CSM decisions from the selected-segment diagnostic runs, which are separate from the main runs.
For each method, exactly the same target decisions are scored against both label versions; rescoring preserves the recorded outputs.
Like the main experiment, this diagnostic uses \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} through the official OpenAI API, with temperature zero and thinking disabled.
Decision-output limits are 256 tokens for Sports/Home and 512 for Code.
Both methods retain the 72-hour/100-event bound and episode control; ProactMem additionally retains daily validation and replay gating.

Tables~\ref{tab:paired_label_f1_450} and~\ref{tab:label_review_450} retain the paired scores and confusion counts underlying Section~\ref{sec:reviewed_diagnostics}.
The diagnostic segment and recorded outputs are fixed; the main evaluation uses its own full-scope events and runs.
Across scopes, immediate and scheduled service decisions share the \textsc{Intervene} label.
The paired episode analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:episode_review_450} uses references grounded in these reviewed events and their causal histories, with its annotation status specified below.

\subsection{Episode References, Matching Rules, and Counts}
\label{app:episode_review_450}

On the same 450 targets and 392 preceding events, we compare recorded CSM and \system{} decisions using a common set of 67 proposed episodes: 26 in Sports, 10 in Home, and 31 in Code.
The analysis separates first effective assistance, useful follow-up, and repetition, using fixed recorded outputs and event labels.
Each episode specifies a service goal and object, causal evidence, onset, valid observed event positions, and any explicit deadline or closure evidence.
This post-hoc paired diagnostic uses model-assisted episode references and semantic matches, assessed separately from the blinded event-label review. Six Home follow-up classifications have human confirmation; the remaining episode and matching proposals retain provisional status.
Validity is assessed at the listed observations in each episode reference.

\paragraph{Reference and model histories.}
Fixed event labels use reference-opportunity novelty (Equation~\ref{eq:or_semantics}); episode coverage follows the evaluated method's actual preceding interventions.
Each method initializes coverage from its own prefix outputs. Scoring counts only target interventions, and each method maintains an independent coverage history.
A target intervention must match an episode's service goal and object at a listed valid observation.
For timeliness, intended service time is the decision time plus the specified delay, with zero delay for immediate intervention.
If an explicit reference deadline exists, coverage credit requires the intended service time to meet that deadline.
The first timely match covers the episode.
For subsequent matches, we compare the message and available evidence with that method's preceding assistance for the same episode.
A follow-up is valuable when new causal evidence changes the actionable service or next step and the message addresses that change; rephrasing the same advice or continued status alone is redundant.
Cases requiring unobserved delivery, recipient response, or task completion are left uncertain.
Valuable follow-ups contribute to service quality within the existing episode; coverage counts each episode once.
Each output credits at most one episode: among compatible references, the first uncovered one in the fixed reference order takes precedence; otherwise the first already covered one is used.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Let $I$ be all target interventions, $F$ first valid interventions, $V$ valuable follow-ups, and $R$ redundant follow-ups.
Episode Coverage is covered new episodes divided by all new episodes; its unit is the service episode, whereas main-table Coverage uses reference-positive events.
Supported Relevance (SR) is $(F+V)/I$, extending the first-only Episode Relevance $F/I$ while retaining single-credit episode coverage.
Redundant Intervention Rate (RIR) is $R/I$.
All target interventions contribute to $I$ in SR and RIR, including uncertain follow-ups ($U$), unmatched outputs, late outputs, and unresolved matches.
Their numerators count supported assistance ($F+V$) and redundant follow-ups ($R$), respectively; Episode Coverage uses the number of new reference episodes as its denominator.
These categories describe compatibility with the proposed references. Recipient benefit requires prospective validation.
Table~\ref{tab:episode_review_450} retains coverage and rates, and Table~\ref{tab:episode_categories_450} retains their count decomposition.

\paragraph{Observation boundaries.}
The main diagnostic treats the first Home opportunity as target-onset; a plausible continuation from the prefix changes both methods' denominator to nine, with CSM covering 7/9 and \system{} 9/9.
Coverage describes the observed segment; lifetime coverage requires follow-up beyond that segment.
Code has six late CSM outputs and four late \system{} outputs; scheduled decisions are assessed using their intended service times and explicit deadlines.
Three CSM Code follow-ups have uncertain value: accepting all three would raise its SR from 33.8\% to 38.5\%, a sensitivity scenario for those three judgments.
Alternative service interpretations of other unmatched and unresolved outputs remain open for review.
