\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The benchmark contains 3,080 decision events derived from real longitudinal traces across two domains, 13 isolated streams, and 423 entity-days.
This supports the controlled comparisons reported here but does not establish universal proactive-agent performance.
HeartSteps covers optional activity support and CASAS covers gentle ambient-sensing check-ins.
Open-ended personal assistance, workplace coordination, and high-risk health or safety interventions remain outside scope.

The reference labels operationalize reviewable service opportunities rather than objectively optimal actions or causal treatment effects.
They were produced through LLM-assisted screening, independent annotation by three team members conducted in parallel with but isolated from model inference, cross-review, adjudication, and an episode-aware freeze.
Because the released benchmark freezes the adjudicated reference labels rather than a separately preserved annotation-reliability study, we do not infer an agreement statistic from the final labels.
A broader user study could test whether recipients agree with the timing, content, and interruption cost of the frozen opportunities.

We use a protocol-fixed evaluation rather than a separate development/test split.
The architecture was developed first, and the benchmark rows, reference labels, prompt, Source bound, and replay gate were locked before the first reported benchmark inference and were not tuned afterward.
This design supports a controlled comparison under one frozen protocol, but does not estimate performance after model selection on an independent development set.
The 50-minute CASAS aggregation was likewise fixed before benchmark construction to preserve two-week chronology at manageable evaluation cost; we do not claim invariance to other aggregation widths.

All main experiments use one LLM configuration to isolate architectural effects.
We do not test a second model or the released PA checkpoint.
The controlled Source/Working/Long-term comparison supports the architectural claim within this model setting, but cross-model robustness remains future work.
Similarly, the validation audit establishes traceability and replay consistency, not that every accepted trajectory is the only valid semantic abstraction.

Finally, offline reference labels cannot measure satisfaction, long-term behavior change, or deployment safety.
Prospective evaluation is needed for user control, correction, personalization, and long-run trust.
