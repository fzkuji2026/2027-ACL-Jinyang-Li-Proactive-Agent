\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied proactive memory as a decision problem rather than retrieval followed by unconditional injection.
\system{} discovers persistent habits from repeated interaction evidence and separately controls whether a habit should be surfaced through Generalized Habit Matching, Code-specific phase/Think checks, and per-habit cooldown.
On the behavior-only benchmark, its matched-input habit F1 is 0.893, compared with 0.828 for Direct Summary, 0.700 for Structured Conditional Discovery, and 0.553 for Mem0 Extraction; repeated runs confirm the same direction.
Across six domains, \system{} improves average F1 over implementation-controlled memory baselines and pinned Mem0/ProactiveAgent artifacts, while the source-disjoint blind holdout reproduces the aggregate advantage under matched raw input, model budget, and cooldown.
Factorial attribution, profile controls, and condition-selectivity analysis identify complementary roles: Habit Induction improves evidence-grounded representation, the intervention Controller raises actionable recall, and cooldown regulates intervention cost.
This separation offers a practical direction for web agents and intelligent assistants that must learn from longitudinal behavior without turning every related memory into an interruption.
