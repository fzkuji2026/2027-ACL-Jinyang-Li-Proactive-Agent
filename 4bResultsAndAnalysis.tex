\subsection{Model comparison (RQ2)}
\label{sec:model_sensitivity}

\paragraph{Within-series model scaling.}
Construction and decision LLMs change together.
Table~\ref{tab:backbone_sensitivity} keeps the benchmark, architecture, role-specific prompts, decoding, validation thresholds, and controller policy fixed.
The comparison shows increasing effectiveness and decreasing intrusion from GPT-5.6 Luna through GPT-5.6 Terra to GPT-5.6 Sol in every scope.
These gains are consistent with stronger abilities to extract task-relevant details, induce conditional behavioral regularities, and perform compositional reasoning over temporal evidence, supporting more accurate memory construction and more selective intervention.
Our hierarchical memory architecture engages these abilities in both historical representation and current decision-making.
Controlled ablations retain GPT-5.6 Luna.

\paragraph{Cross-family event-only comparison.}
We compare GPT-5.6 Luna, Gemini 3.8 Flash, DeepSeek V4 Flash, and GLM 5.3 Flash on two selected complete event streams per scope, using shared task instructions, bounded event context, and intervention control (Figure~\ref{fig:event_only_model_radar}; settings in Appendix~\ref{app:cross_family_streams}).
On Sports, Gemini and Luna share the highest coverage, while Gemini leads the remaining metrics.
On Home and Code, Gemini leads in effectiveness; Luna recovers more opportunities and achieves higher balance.
DeepSeek and GLM have higher specificity and lower coverage than Luna in these two scopes.
The scope-specific profiles reveal different trade-offs between opportunity recovery and correct silence.

\subsection{Ablation study and memory analysis (RQ3)}
\label{sec:hierarchy}
\label{sec:validation_ablations}

\paragraph{Proactive agent ablation.}
The event-only variant supplies current and bounded recent events; the event and trajectory memory variant adds source-checked temporal trajectories; \system{} adds behavioral regularities.
These three variants in Figure~\ref{fig:memory_hierarchy} share Luna, decision prompt, schema, event bound, and controller.
The controller uses representation-dependent keys, so the comparison measures memory-layer contributions within the implemented pipeline.
The temporal trajectories recover more opportunities, most on Code, with unchanged false-positive counts on Sports and Home and more false positives on Code.
The behavioral regularities further improve effectiveness in every scope, recovering more opportunities with fewer false interventions (RQ3).
Trajectory abstraction expands context; supported, currently applicable regularities improve selectivity.

\paragraph{Validation mechanisms.}
Table~\ref{tab:validation_ablations} fixes model, prompt, event bound, context capacity, and controller.
\emph{w/o replay gate} bypasses activation thresholds for well-formed, source-linked, online-reconstructable candidates, retaining current-applicability matching.
\emph{w/o trajectory validation} stores schema-valid, same-entity trajectory extractions without fact checking, retaining weekly construction and replay.
Both removals lower relevance and effectiveness (RQ3).
Replay gating contributes more effectiveness and improves coverage in every scope.
On Code, trajectory validation improves selectivity with fewer false interventions and unchanged true-positive counts.

\paragraph{Intervention control.}
The before suppression diagnostic scores recorded Code proposals while holding later states and decisions fixed.
Filtering removes more false-positive than true-positive proposals, yielding higher relevance and effectiveness alongside lower coverage.
Appendix~\ref{app:extended_ablations} reports expanded ablation and intervention control scores.
