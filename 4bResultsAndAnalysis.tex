\paragraph{Reference sensitivity.}
\label{sec:reviewed_diagnostics}
Rescoring fixed outputs from separate 450-target runs against the two reference versions (Section~\ref{sec:reference_labels}) preserves \system{}'s Effectiveness advantage over CSM, our recursive-summary control (Section~\ref{sec:recursive_summary}), on Home/Code; the small Sports ranking reverses.

\paragraph{Episode-level boundary.}
A model-assisted diagnostic on those targets defines 67 goal-and-object service episodes.
First timely matching assistance covers an episode; repetition without actionable new evidence is redundant.
\system{}/CSM cover 23/26 Sports episodes each, 10/10 versus 8/10 on Home, and 14/31 versus 18/31 on Code.
On Code, \system{} uses fewer interventions (42 versus 65), but its redundant shares are higher in all scopes: 1.2/43.6/9.5\% versus CSM's 1.1/32.1/4.6\% (Sports/Home/Code).
Only six Home follow-up classifications are human-confirmed; other references and matches remain provisional.
Event-level gains do not establish uniformly better episode coverage, repetition control, or recipient benefit.

\subsection{Comparison of Memory Strategies}
\label{sec:recursive_summary}

\paragraph{Recursive-summary control.}
Cross-week Summary Memory (CSM) summarizes each completed day's committed events, then recursively merges seven observed daily records with its previous cross-week summary; this can span more than seven calendar days.
Decisions receive current/recent events, unconsolidated daily summaries, and the cross-week summary.
CSM checks format/length; \system{} also checks source facts, replays support, and selects applicable patterns.
Both share Luna, streams, labels, the 72-hour/100-event bound, task policy, output contract, and controller.
Memory guidance and construction budgets differ, making this a complete-strategy control rather than a component ablation (RQ2).

Against CSM, \system{} has higher Relevance, Effectiveness, and Balance, and lower FPR across scopes (Figure~\ref{fig:recursive_summary}).
Code Effectiveness rises 43.9\% to 55.4\% and FPR falls 32.1\% to 18.1\%, but Coverage falls 94.8\% to 89.7\%: 179 fewer false interventions accompany nine fewer true positives.
IR falls on Sports/Code but rises on Home; gains are not simply reduced intervention frequency.
Appendix~\ref{app:configuration} details CSM's configuration; Table~\ref{tab:recursive_summary_full} reports its complete scores.
