\paragraph{Overall comparison.}
At a 34.8\% micro Push rate, Full \system{} reaches 39.2\% precision, 75.7\% recall, and 51.7\% F1.
Relative to PA-Prompt, it reduces Push rate by 2.7 points while improving precision by 8.7 points, recall by 12.3 points, and F1 by 10.5 points.
The gain is therefore not produced by indiscriminate intervention.
PA-Prompt exposes a long causal history but leaves the model to recover its temporal structure at every turn; \system{} supplies a smaller, validated representation of that structure.

We additionally conduct an entity-composition sensitivity check, reported only
in the supplementary material.

\paragraph{HeartSteps.}
PA-Prompt obtains the highest recall (82.5\%) but Pushes on 69.0\% of all events.
Full \system{} lowers the Push rate by 15.2 points, increases precision from 30.0\% to 35.8\%, and improves F1 from 43.9\% to 48.8\%.
The result reflects a useful trade-off for optional activity support: validated history removes many generic suggestions while preserving most reference opportunities.

\paragraph{CASAS.}
The prompt baseline is conservative but misses nearly three quarters of reference opportunities, reaching only 26.8\% recall.
Full \system{} raises recall to 73.7\% and precision to 48.6\%, yielding 58.6\% F1 versus 30.1\% for PA-Prompt.
Each public row contains only the current 50-minute segment, so the improvement depends on Agent-maintained cross-segment evidence rather than a benchmark-provided anomaly feature.
These findings answer RQ1.

\paragraph{Auxiliary flat-retrieval check.}
Flat Retrieval Memory reaches 34.2\% Push rate, 33.1\% precision, 62.9\% recall, and 43.4\% micro F1 on the same 3,080 events.
It improves over Source-only by 4.6 F1 points and over PA-Prompt by 2.2 points, showing that retrieving older same-entity events is useful.
However, at nearly the same Push rate as Full \system{} (34.2\% versus 34.8\%), it remains lower by 6.1 precision points, 12.8 recall points, and 8.3 F1 points; its HeartSteps and CASAS F1 scores are 41.3\% and 48.2\%, respectively, versus 48.8\% and 58.6\% for Full \system{}.
Thus, ordinary semantic retrieval provides a stronger history baseline but does not reproduce the benefit of fact-validated daily evidence and replay-validated long-term trajectories.

\subsection{Contribution of the Memory Hierarchy}
\label{sec:hierarchy}

Table~\ref{tab:hierarchy_counts} gives the micro confusion matrices for the controlled hierarchy.
Adding Working Memory yields a net increase of 19 TP without a net increase in FP.
Adding Long-term Memory then yields a net increase of 90 TP and a net reduction of 87 FP, while the total number of Pushes changes by only three.
The long-term level therefore does more than add triggers: its temporal and episode evidence can both reveal previously invisible opportunities and disambiguate unsupported current-event interpretations.

\subsection{Validation and Causal Availability}
\label{sec:validation_results}

We audit the naturally produced memory artifacts without rerunning the 3,080 decisions with validators removed.
This directly tests the two claims made by the architecture: retained daily facts must be source-supported, and retained long-term proposals must be source-linked and evaluated by historical replay before activation.

The daily store contains 423 daily trajectories (367 HeartSteps and 56 CASAS) composed of 1,279 retained episodes.
Every retained episode is traceable to source-level evidence from the same entity, while the construction log shows that the validator rejected 311 unsupported candidates before storage.
The final long-term repository contains 54 source-linked, replay-audited proposals: 42 meet the Active gate and 12 remain Candidate because their replay support is insufficient; only Active trajectories are eligible for retrieval into decision Evidence Cards.
The maintenance log contains 140 replay attempts across successive weekly updates, of which 22 did not pass the Active gate.
Thus, the perfect audit rate of the final store is a consequence of filtering, not evidence that the construction LLM never proposes an unsupported item.

Working Memory becomes available for all 13 entities after a median of 0.80 days.
At least one Active long-term trajectory is available for 11 entities after a median of 6.79 days, and relevant long-term evidence is actually matched for eight entities after a median of 10.46 days.
These boundaries confirm that the benchmark's 41--55-day HeartSteps streams and 14-day CASAS streams are long enough for both daily and weekly levels to affect later decisions.
They also prevent a trajectory from being supported by the event it is used to judge.
The audit answers RQ3.

Three limitations dominate the errors.
First, present intent is underdetermined by both mobile context and ambient sensing, so a plausible message may not match the reviewed opportunity.
Second, a trajectory can be valid yet still fail the present-utility test; the decision model sometimes over-interprets regularity.
Third, episode boundaries are semantic: a later event may contain enough new evidence for a fresh Push even when it resembles a covered window.
These observations answer RQ4 and motivate better evidence-grounded episode tracking rather than a benchmark-specific opportunity threshold.
