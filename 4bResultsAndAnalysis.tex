\subsection{Main results (RQ1)}
\label{sec:main_results}

ProactiveStream evaluates service decisions as history accumulates.

\paragraph{Comparisons with external methods.}
\system{} achieves the highest effectiveness and balance in all three scopes (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, RQ1).

\paragraph{Event-history methods.}
ProactiveAgent intervenes frequently on Sports and Code (2.75$\times$ and 4.45$\times$), which yields high coverage but also high intrusion (64.5 and 49.7); on Home, it recovers only 26.8\% of opportunities.
ProactiveAgent and ContextAgent judge each event from event history and do not focus on when a behavior usually occurs.
Without this information, they cannot tell whether the current moment matches a recurring behavior, so they either intervene broadly or miss opportunities supported by earlier observations.
This shows the need for memory that captures recurring behavior.

\paragraph{Retrieval-based memory.}
Mem0 shows the most unstable trade-off: it recovers 97.8\% of Sports opportunities with 81.0 intrusion, but only 17.4\% and 25.9\% on Home and Code.
A-MEM, the strongest baseline on Home and Code, still trails \system{} by 15.7 and 11.7 effectiveness points.
These methods retrieve memories by semantic relevance, so the retrieved memories reflect what is similar to the current event rather than what applies now.
Their decisions therefore depend on which memories are retrieved, and the trade-off shifts across scopes.
This shows the importance of checking whether historical memory applies at the current moment.

\paragraph{\system{}.}
\system{} has the highest relevance in all three scopes and the highest coverage on Home and Code (73.7 and 89.7).
It uses a regularity only when its trigger and time window match the current event and the expected behavior has not yet occurred, so historical evidence is added at matching moments and left out elsewhere.
As a result, coverage and relevance improve together.
On Sports, intrusion remains 46.1, leaving room for more selective decisions.

\subsection{Model comparison (RQ2)}
\label{sec:model_sensitivity}

\paragraph{Within-series model scaling.}
Construction and decision LLMs change together, with all other settings fixed (Table~\ref{tab:backbone_sensitivity}).
Effectiveness increases and intrusion decreases from GPT-5.6 Luna through GPT-5.6 Terra to GPT-5.6 Sol across scopes.
\system{} engages these backbones in extracting task-relevant details, inducing conditional regularities, and interpreting historical memory alongside current evidence.
The trend is consistent with stronger backbones benefiting this coupled construction-and-decision process.
Controlled ablations retain GPT-5.6 Luna.

\paragraph{Cross-family event-only comparison.}
Using the shared event-only interface, we compare GPT-5.6 Luna, Gemini 3.8 Flash, DeepSeek V4 Flash, and GLM 5.3 Flash on two selected complete streams per scope (Figure~\ref{fig:event_only_model_radar}; Appendix~\ref{app:cross_family_streams}).
The results reveal scope-dependent trade-offs between opportunity recovery and correct abstention.
On Home and Code, Luna recovers more opportunities than DeepSeek and GLM, while the latter achieve higher specificity.
The profiles shift on Sports, linking backbone choice to the service domain and the relative importance of missed opportunities and unnecessary interventions.

\subsection{Ablation and memory analysis (RQ3)}
\label{sec:hierarchy}
\label{sec:validation_ablations}

\paragraph{Proactive agent ablation.}
Figure~\ref{fig:memory_hierarchy} compares event-only memory, event and trajectory memory, and the full hierarchy using Luna under shared decision settings, with representation-dependent intervention keys.
Trajectories extend activity context beyond recent events, while regularities organize recurring evidence around its conditions of applicability.
Opportunity recovery increases with trajectory memory, especially on Code; the full hierarchy combines further recovery with fewer false interventions across scopes.
This progression is consistent with the complementary roles of activity-level context and conditional behavioral evidence in the complete system (RQ3).

\paragraph{Memory validation.}
Source validation checks trajectory fidelity; replay assesses historical support for conditional regularities.
Table~\ref{tab:validation_ablations} compares both ablations with the same model and decision settings.
\emph{w/o replay gate} retains current matching and bypasses activation thresholds for well-formed, source-linked candidates reconstructed online.
\emph{w/o trajectory validation} retains schema-valid same-entity extractions, weekly construction, and replay, omitting fact checks.
Both reduce relevance and effectiveness; removing replay yields the larger effectiveness decline.
On Code, trajectory validation is associated with fewer false interventions at unchanged coverage (RQ3).

\paragraph{Intervention control.}
The Code analysis reveals a trade-off between coverage and selectivity.
On recorded proposals, suppression removes more false positives than true positives, improving relevance and effectiveness while reducing coverage.
Subsequent states and decisions remain fixed (Appendix~\ref{app:extended_ablations}).
